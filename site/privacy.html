Privacy

Your data, plainly explained.

Effective Date: November 16, 2026

Introduction

This Privacy Policy explains what PlantAdvocate ("the App," "we," "us") collects, why, who else receives it, and what you can do about it. It covers the PlantAdvocate iPhone app and this website, plantadvocate.ai. PlantAdvocate is operated by Allison Bowman, a sole proprietor, based in the United States.

The short version: PlantAdvocate is free. It has no ads, no tracking, no analytics or crash-reporting software, no in-app purchases and no subscriptions. We don't sell your data, and we don't share it with advertisers or data brokers.

If you have questions about this policy or your data, contact us at support@plantadvocate.ai.

Information We Collect

Account information. You sign in with Apple or Google. There are no passwords. From Apple we receive an account identifier, your email address (or Apple's private relay address, if you chose Hide My Email; we respect that choice), and your name, but only the first time you sign in and only if you share it. From Google we receive an account identifier, your verified email address and your name. We don't keep your Google profile photo. We give your account its own random identifier, which ties your plants, care logs and growing areas to you. If you sign in with Apple and with Google using the same verified email address, both open the same account. For Sign in with Apple we also keep, encrypted, a token Apple issues to us. We use it only to end PlantAdvocate's access to your Apple ID when you delete your account. We use account information only to run the App. We never use it for marketing or advertising, and we never add the address you sign in with to a mailing list. We send news about PlantAdvocate, such as its launch, only to people who asked for it, like those on the early-access list described under "This Website" below.

Plants and growing areas. The App keeps what you tell it about your plants: nicknames, species, how many, growth stage, the spot you describe (such as "by the south fence"), when you got each plant and what shape it was in, notes you write, a log of the care you give (watering, feeding, repotting and the like) with its dates and outcomes, and moves between growing areas. It also keeps your growing areas: their names and the way you describe them, such as their type, how sheltered and sunny they are, and an optional size and goals. All of this is stored on our server, so your garden survives a new phone and advice can account for what you've actually done. Care reminders and seasonal alerts are scheduled on your phone. Our server sends no notifications, and we keep no notification token for your phone.

Your land. Adding your land is optional, and the rest of the App works without it. If you add it, the App keeps the pin you place on your home, as exact coordinates, and whether it came from your phone's location or from you placing it. It keeps a name for the land, what you tell us is on it, and whether frost comes earlier or later in your yard. It keeps each zone you add. A zone is a part of your land, drawn as a box on the aerial image, and we keep the box, what grows there and whether those plants drop their leaves. It keeps every correction you make to a value (such as sun, soil, a soil test number, or where water stands), including a correction you later take back. It keeps what you tell us about the season: that a frost happened and on which day, that it hasn't happened yet at your place, or that a task is done or not needed. It also keeps what our server worked out for your land from public maps, such as its hardiness zone, soil, slope, sun and where water collects, together with the outline of the area each value was worked out for, and a note when a forecast calls for the fall's first frost or freeze. When a value is worked out again, for example after you move the pin or redraw a zone, the earlier value is kept as well, marked as replaced, with the block and outline it was worked out for. Each zone is kept as a growing area, with a copy of the land's pin and town. We fill in the land's nearest town, state and time zone from the U.S. National Weather Service's answer for the rounded point described under "Weather," and set its country to the United States when that service covers it. We never receive or keep a street address or postal code for your land. Your pin, zone boxes, corrections and seasonal answers are never included in the community census.

Location. The App uses location in 2 ways, a growing area's place and your land's pin. Both are optional. The App asks for location access only when you tap to use your phone's current location, and only while you're using the App.

A growing area's place. You can give a growing area a place, so the App knows the weather where your plants grow. You can type an address or use your phone's current location. Your phone's own map service (Apple's, on iPhone) turns that into a place. We store the place's city, region, country and coordinates with that growing area. We don't keep the address you typed. Growing areas work without a place. Coordinates are never included in the community census.

Your land's pin. To add your land, you type an address or use your phone's current location. A typed address goes to your phone's own map service (Apple's, on iPhone) to be turned into a spot on the map. It is never sent to us. The App sends that spot to our server. Our server gets the aerial image of the block of land around it, from its own copy or from the U.S. Geological Survey as "Public maps for your land" describes, shows you that image, and does not keep the spot. You then put a pin on your home in that image. The pin you confirm is stored on our server exactly as you placed it.

Public maps for your land. Our server never gives your pin to anyone. To read your land, it asks public map services about a block of land about 1,600 feet (500 meters) across (narrower from east to west in Alaska) that contains your pin. The block is taken from a fixed grid: it is the block whose center is the grid point nearest your pin. So a service can work out that your pin is in the middle part of the block, within about 500 feet (150 meters) of its center in each direction, but not where in that middle part. These requests come from our server, not from your phone, and carry nothing else about you. The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Natural Resources Conservation Service is asked for the block's soil survey. The U.S. Geological Survey is asked for the block's elevation, its land cover and tree cover, and its aerial image (National Agriculture Imagery Program photography). For the aerial image, our server first looks for its own copy, and asks the U.S. Geological Survey only when it has no copy or its copy has reached its age limit. That can happen when you look up your land or move its pin, when our server reads the public maps for your land, and when Home, Your land or the zone map shows the map of your land. When our server reads the public maps for your land, it can also ask that service for the date the photograph was taken. Amazon Web Services, which hosts the canopy height map published by Meta and the World Resources Institute, is asked for the part of that map that covers the block. The hardiness zone and typical frost dates are looked up in files on our own server, so nothing is sent anywhere for them. None of these services is asked about a place outside the United States. Our server tells what is in the United States from a rough outline, so a place just across the Canadian or Mexican border, in Windsor or Ciudad Juárez for example, is treated as inside it, and a few places just inside the border, and a few remote U.S. islands such as the western Aleutians and Midway, are treated as outside it.

Map layers. The map of your land on Home and Your land can show 3 layers over the aerial image: Sun, Trees and Water. Our server draws each one as a picture of the whole block from the copies of public elevation and canopy height data it already holds for that block, as "Copies of public data" describes, and, for Sun, the typical frost dates in its own files. Drawing them sends nothing to anyone outside our server: no map service, weather service or AI service is asked anything for them. If a copy a layer needs is missing, the layer is not drawn, and our server does not ask a map service for that copy just to draw it. A layer covers the whole block, so it is the same for everyone whose land is in that block, and it is filed with nothing that says whose land is there.

Weather. To get a forecast, our server sends coordinates rounded to about two-thirds of a mile (a kilometer), and nothing else about you, to a weather service. For the Home report and the seasonal plan, for a place in the United States (by the same rough outline), it asks the U.S. National Weather Service first. When Apple Weather is available, it is also asked about the same rounded point, for a second opinion on overnight lows on your land. Its answer is kept only in our server's memory, never in our database, and is reused for 3 hours. It stays in memory until that point is asked about again or the server restarts. If the National Weather Service has no forecast to give for a place in the United States, the Home report asks Apple Weather alone, and the seasonal plan shows typical dates without a forecast. If the National Weather Service says a place is outside the area it covers, and the place is not in a United States territory, the Home report gets that place's weather as it would for a place outside the United States. This changes only the weather: the public map services are still asked as "Public maps for your land" describes. For a growing area's own weather, including a zone's and the weather behind an outdoor plant's care advice, and for the Home report for a place outside the United States, our server asks Apple Weather first. If it doesn't answer, it asks the U.S. National Weather Service, which answers only for places in the United States, then Open-Meteo. The seasonal plan for land outside the United States asks no weather service.

Copies of public data. So that we don't ask the same thing twice, we keep a copy of what the public map services returned for a block, including its aerial image, and of the National Weather Service's forecast for a rounded point. We also keep the Sun, Trees and Water pictures our server draws for a block, as "Map layers" describes. These copies are filed by area, with nothing that says whose land is there. People whose land is in the same block share a map copy, and people near the same rounded point share a forecast copy. A forecast copy lasts 3 hours. A map copy is deleted when it reaches its age limit or when no land in our records is in its block, whichever comes first. The age limit is at most 90 days for soil and elevation, and a year for land cover, canopy height and the aerial image. A map layer picture's age limit is never later than that of the copies it was drawn from, so it is deleted with them or sooner, and a picture is drawn again when a copy it was drawn from is newer. An aerial image is kept for 2 hours after it was fetched, so it is there while you place your pin, unless deleting an account clears its block sooner. A cleanup that runs about once an hour removes the copies whose time is up.

Camera and photos. On Walk the garden, the camera shows your garden behind your list. Nothing it sees is recorded, kept or sent. This version of the App does not offer identifying a plant from a photo. If it returns, your phone's own AI (Apple Intelligence, on iPhone) will read the photo on the phone, and the photo will not be uploaded, to us or to anyone else.

Photo check-ups and questions in your own words. These 2 features are not switched on today, and the App doesn't show them. When they are switched on, a photo check-up sends that one photo, with any note you add, to our server and on to our AI provider, Anthropic, to read it. A question in your own words goes the same way. We don't store the photo. Only the written result of a check-up is saved, to that plant's timeline. A question and its answer are not saved. The App will ask you before it sends your first photo or question. "AI Processing" below lists exactly what goes with them.

Voice. In Walk the garden you can say what's planted instead of typing it. The App asks for microphone and speech-recognition access when you first tap the microphone. It listens only while the screen says "Listening," and stops when you tap stop or leave the screen. Your phone turns your speech into text, on the phone itself where it can. Where it can't, iOS sends the audio to Apple's speech service to be transcribed, and the screen tells you which is happening. PlantAdvocate never receives your audio or the transcript. Only the plants you confirm (the species, how many, and any spot you named) are sent to our server, and they are saved like any plant you add by hand.

Community census. The community census is off by default. Your plantings are counted only if you turn on "Join the community census" in Settings → Privacy & data. If you join, each of your plantings is counted with its species, how many, its growth stage, the month you got it, the starting condition you picked from fixed lists (soil moisture, leaves, pests), and how many times each kind of care was logged in each calendar month. It also carries the type of growing area it's in and that area's city, region and country, the months it moved between growing areas and the type of each one, and, if it was split off another planting, which one. Within one export, plantings in the same growing area are marked as sharing it. The census never contains your name, email address, account identifier, plant nicknames, notes you wrote, photos, coordinates or the day you gave any care. Every identifier in a census export is made new for that export, and the plantings are listed in a random order, so no identifier links one export to another. That is not a promise that 2 exports can never be matched: a planting's monthly care counts could still look like the same planting in a later export. Only PlantAdvocate can export census data; other users can't see it. We use it to improve care advice for everyone. It is not sold and not shared with advertisers. Because it describes single plantings rather than totals, someone who already knew your city and exactly what you grow could in principle recognize your garden in it. If that matters to you, leave the census off. Turning it off leaves your plantings out of every export after that. An export made before you turned it off can't be called back.

Messages you send us. If you email us, or tap "Report this result" in the App (it opens an email already filled in with the result you're reporting and the App's version, which you see before you send it), we receive what you send and your email address. We use them only to reply and to fix problems.

Technical data. The App contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting software. It doesn't send us usage statistics, device identifiers or crash reports. Our server keeps operational logs so we can keep the service running: the time of each request, which part of the service it asked for, and the network (IP) address it came from. A request line or an error detail can sometimes include a plant's nickname, an internal number for your land, a location rounded to about two-thirds of a mile (a kilometer), or which block of land about 1,600 feet (500 meters) across an aerial image was for. A request for a map layer, or a note that one could not be drawn or stored, can include that internal number for your land and which layer it was (Sun, Trees or Water), but not the block. The App adds the block's name to a request for a layer picture, and our logs leave that part of the request out. Our logs do not record your pin, or the spot the App sends before you place it. We don't use these logs to profile you.

AI Processing

On your phone. The Gnome's way of talking uses your phone's own AI. Nothing is sent anywhere for it.

On our server. Care advice is built on our server, by fixed rules, from the catalog's care facts and your plant's own record. Your land's profile and the seasonal plan are also built on our server by fixed rules, from public maps, the forecast and what you tell us, and the map layers are drawn there by fixed rules from public maps. An outside AI service does not write any of them.

Not switched on yet. Photo check-ups and questions in your own words will use Anthropic's Claude to read the photo or the question. Today no feature of PlantAdvocate sends anything to Anthropic or to any other outside AI service. When these features are switched on, we will send only what the answer needs: the photo or question and any note you add, the species' care facts, and the plant's nickname and the spot you described. A question also carries the plant's growth stage and its recent care log, including notes you wrote, and for an outdoor plant, the growing area's name, how exposed it is, and the local forecast. We never send your name, email address, account identifier or coordinates. Anthropic handles this under its commercial terms, which don't allow it to train its models on what we send. The App will ask you before your first photo or question goes.

Care advice is informational and can be wrong. It is not a substitute for a professional's advice, and you should never rely on it to decide whether a plant is safe to eat or touch. You can report a plant or species result that looks wrong from inside the App. To report a result about your land or the season, email us.

How We Use Information

We use the information described above to:

  • Run the App: keep your plants, care logs, growing areas and land; build care advice, your land's profile, its map layers and the seasonal plan; and fetch local weather and public map data for the places you give
  • Count the community census, only if you've joined it, to improve care advice for everyone
  • Answer your messages and reports
  • Keep your account secure, and find and fix problems

We don't use your information for advertising, and we don't build a profile of you.

Data Storage and Security

Your data is stored in the United States, on servers run by our hosting provider, Fly.io, on storage that is encrypted at rest. It travels between the App and our server over encrypted (HTTPS) connections, and your sign-in is kept in your phone's secure storage. We take reasonable steps to protect your information, but no way of storing or sending data is completely secure.

Data Sharing

We don't sell your personal information, and we don't share it for advertising. We share only what each of these service providers needs to do its job:

  • Fly.io hosts our server and stores the App's data.
  • Apple and Google confirm your sign-in. On iPhone, Apple also turns addresses into places, and transcribes speech when your phone can't do it on its own.
  • Apple Weather, the U.S. National Weather Service and Open-Meteo receive coordinates rounded to about two-thirds of a mile (a kilometer) to return a forecast, in the order "Weather" describes.
  • The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Natural Resources Conservation Service and the U.S. Geological Survey receive the block of land about 1,600 feet (500 meters) across that contains your pin, to return its soil survey, elevation, land cover, tree cover and aerial image. Before you place your pin, the U.S. Geological Survey also receives the block around the spot you look up, to return its aerial image.
  • Amazon Web Services hosts the canopy height map published by Meta and the World Resources Institute. Our server asks it for the part of that map that covers the block.
  • Anthropic will receive what "AI Processing" describes, and only once photo check-ups or questions in your own words are switched on.
  • Cloudflare serves this website.

If you've joined the community census, your census data is used as described above. Before census data goes to any other organization, we will name it here. We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

Keeping and Deleting Your Data

We keep your data while your account exists. You can delete your account at any time in the App: Settings → Account → Delete account. That immediately and permanently erases your account, plants, care logs, growing areas and land from our live database, including your land's pin, zones, corrections, seasonal answers and everything worked out for it. Deleting your account also ends your sign-in sessions and, if you used Sign in with Apple, asks Apple to end PlantAdvocate's access to your Apple ID. If Apple doesn't accept that request straight away, we keep the encrypted Apple sign-in grant, with nothing that says whose it was, for up to 30 days after the deletion, only to ask Apple again, and then delete it. Our hosting provider keeps automatic daily snapshots of our storage for up to 5 days, so a deleted account is gone from those within 5 days. Copies of public data are filed by area, not by account, and are cleared as "Copies of public data" describes. A census export made before you deleted your account can't be called back. If you prefer, email us and we'll delete your account for you. This version of the App has no button for removing your land while keeping your account, so email us for that too. When we remove your land but keep your account, everything kept for your land is erased, except that a zone that holds plants, or once held them, stays as an ordinary growing area. It keeps its name, what grows there, whether those plants drop their leaves and any sun correction you made, with the pin's coordinates and town as its place. Its box is erased.

Your Rights and Choices

Wherever you live, you can ask us for a copy of your data, or ask us to correct or delete it. Email us at the address above. If you live somewhere with specific privacy laws, such as the EU, the UK or California, those rights are yours by law, including the right to complain to your local data protection authority. We won't treat you differently for using them.

The community census stays off unless you turn it on, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy & data.

You can decline, or later turn off, camera, photo library, location, microphone and speech-recognition access in your iPhone's Settings. The features that need them will say so and offer another way, such as typing a plant's name or an address.

This Website

plantadvocate.ai has no analytics, ads or tracking. It is served by Cloudflare, which handles your network (IP) address to deliver the pages and protect the site from abuse, and may set a cookie for that protection. The pages load their fonts from Google Fonts, so your browser asks Google for them. The community garden demo saves your plots in your own browser; they never reach us. If you email us from the site, we use your address and message only to reply. If you joined our early-access list on an earlier version of this site, we use that address only to tell you about PlantAdvocate, and we'll remove it if you ask. When we remove an address, we keep only a one-way fingerprint of it. The fingerprint can't be turned back into the address, but it lets us recognize the address if it turns up again, and we use it only to keep that address off anything we send to the list.

Children's Privacy

PlantAdvocate is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we'll delete it.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this Privacy Policy from time to time. When we do, we'll change the effective date and the "What changed" line at the top. The current version always lives at this page. Continued use of the App after changes are posted means you accept the updated policy.
