# App Review risk for PlantAdvocate 1.1.3

This file assesses the risk that App Review rejects PlantAdvocate 1.1.3 on its first submission, starting with the sign-in wall: the app asks for Sign in with Apple or Google before it shows anything, and there is no demo account and no demo mode. It covers what Apple's rules say, what developers have reported from 2024 to 2026, a likelihood for each risk, what `docs/app-store/review-notes.md` already does about it, and the fixes ranked by cost. It was checked on 2026-10-23. The guidelines and Apple's help pages were fetched and read on that day. The forum threads were read through a summarising fetch, so they are paraphrased here, not quoted. The likelihoods are my judgement, built from thin evidence, and the file says where. No code was changed.

Two things changed after it was checked. The review notes were rewritten on 24 October from this file's findings (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md`; its Notes block is 3,822 bytes since 30 October, not the 3,987 described below). And on 30 October 1.1.3 gained the line "These notes repeat what the sources publish. They are not medical or veterinary advice." under each hazard note (`mobile/src/care/notAdvice.ts`), which answers the "Health-adjacent claims" point below. The rest is as checked on 2026-10-23.

## The short answer

The sign-in wall itself is a modest risk, and the review notes already handle most of it. Sign in with Apple is a one-tap sign-in that makes an account for whoever taps it, which is the best case an app with no passwords can offer a reviewer. I found one developer who shipped an Apple-only app with no credentials supplied and reported approval, and I found no 2024 to 2026 report of a rejection whose only stated reason was a missing demo account on an Apple-only app. The reports I did find are about different failures: a Google or Apple test account that stopped at a verification code, Sign in with Apple itself breaking on Apple's side, and apps whose sign-in only worked for accounts made on a website.

The larger risks are around the sign-in rather than the missing demo account. Production does not yet serve the routes the app calls, so a reviewer who signed in today would get an account and then meet failing screens. The Species Almanac is a reference catalog that only signed-in requests can read, and Apple's rule on logins asks that apps let people in without one wherever the feature doesn't need an account. And the notes leave out several things Apple's help page says to include. Taken together, and treating them as independent, I put the chance of at least one rejection of 1.1.3 at roughly one in three. For scale, Apple's own 2025 report counts [2,093,244 rejections against 9,100,620 submissions reviewed](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/), about 23 percent, and a footnote says apps may be submitted more than once before approval, so the count includes resubmissions. A first review of a 140-commit branch that has not been run on a phone ([handoff](../../2026-10-16-app-store-finish-line-handoff.md)) should sit above that average. Most of the mass is in technical and metadata items, not in the sign-in wall.

## What Apple's rules say

The guidelines page was last updated on [October 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), and Apple's [announcement of that update](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i) lists no change to 2.1, 4.8 or 5.1.1. It does add 2.3.14 on creative assets and 2.5.19 on hidden, dormant or undocumented features, which moves a rule that used to live in 2.3.1. The wording below is paraphrased from the live page, so read the sections themselves before relying on a detail.

Guideline 2.1(a), App Completeness, says a submission should be final and tested on a device, with working URLs. If the app has a login, the developer must include demo account information and turn the back end on. If a demo account can't be given because of legal or security obligations, the developer may build in a demo mode instead, but only with Apple's prior approval, and the mode must show the app's full functionality ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple added that demo-mode sentence on [October 24, 2022](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=xk8d7p8c). The same page's Before You Submit list repeats the demand for either an active demo account or a fully featured demo mode wherever the app has account-based features. This is the rule a reviewer cites if sign-in fails or if they can't find a way in.

Apple's help page on [providing information for a complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review/) says what App Review Information should hold. For a new app it asks for the concept and business model, the purpose and audience, the devices and OS versions tested, the external services used (it names authentication services), and any regional differences. For demo accounts it says to enter credentials that work on the day of submission, and that expired or changed credentials may get the submission rejected. It describes a demonstration mode instead of an account for apps that handle sensitive user information or operate in a highly regulated industry. It does not say "only", but those are the cases it names, PlantAdvocate is neither, and guideline 2.1(a) adds the prior-approval condition, so that door is shut to it in practice. The same page says that if an app needs an authentication code as well as or instead of credentials, the code goes in the Notes in advance, and that without it a call may be needed, typically within three to five business days. Apple's [App Store Connect reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/platform-version-information/) says the Notes field holds up to 4000 bytes, and describes the Sign-in required fields as a demo account that must not expire, with single sign-on services such as Facebook given their own demo login. It says nothing specific about Sign in with Apple, which has no password to give.

Guideline 4.8, Login Services, says an app that uses a third-party or social login such as Google Sign-In to set up the primary account must also offer an equivalent login service that limits collection to name and email, lets the user keep their email private, and doesn't collect app interactions for advertising without consent. It lists exemptions for apps that use only their own account system, among others ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). A rejection message quoted in a forum post says that Sign in with Apple meets the requirements of 4.8 ([forum, Oct 2024](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765145)); the quote comes from the poster, not from an Apple reply in the thread. PlantAdvocate offers it first on the login screen (`mobile/src/auth/LoginScreen.tsx`), so 4.8 is met.

Guideline 5.1.1(v), Account Sign-In, is the one with the most room for argument. Its opening sentence tells developers whose apps lack significant account-based features to ["let people use it without a login"](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), which is Apple's wording. It also says that an app which creates accounts must let people delete them in the app, that apps shouldn't require personal information except where it's directly relevant to core function, and that an app whose core isn't tied to a specific social network must give access without a login or by another mechanism. Apple's [account deletion page](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/) adds that apps with Sign in with Apple should revoke tokens through the REST API and use its server-to-server notification endpoints, and that accounts made automatically still need a deletion option.

Guideline 2.3, Accurate Metadata, asks that the metadata, privacy information included, reflect the app's core experience. 2.3.1(a) says new features must be described with specificity in the Notes for Review and be reachable, 2.3.3 says screenshots must show the app in use and not merely a login page, and 2.3.6 asks for honest age-rating answers ([guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Guideline 4.2 asks that an app go beyond a repackaged website, and exempts catalogs from the rule against apps that are mainly collections of links. 4.3(b) names dating, flashlight, sound-effect, wallpaper, simple-timer and fortune-telling apps as saturated and doesn't name plant care. Guideline 1.4.1 puts medical apps that could give inaccurate data, or be used to diagnose or treat patients, under greater scrutiny, and asks them to remind users to check with a doctor. 2.5.19 says apps must not carry hidden, dormant or undocumented features, and that their functionality should be clear to users and to App Review.

Two more facts frame the cost of getting this wrong. Apple says App Review typically looks at [at least half of submissions in under 24 hours and 90 percent in under 48](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/), and that on average [over 40 percent of unresolved issues are 2.1 App Completeness](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/). If a rejection comes, Apple wants a reply in App Store Connect first, and an [appeal only if that doesn't settle it, one appeal per rejected app](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/after-submitting-for-review/appeal-to-the-app-review-board/).

The pattern is clear enough to act on. Handing a reviewer a test Apple or Google account fails when the account asks for a code the reviewer can't receive. Apple's help page says an authentication code the app needs should go in the Notes in advance, but a one-time code that Google or Apple sends at sign-in can't be written down ahead of time, so that advice doesn't settle this case. Letting the reviewer sign in with their own Apple ID, which is what an Apple-only app does by default, is the path with the one reported success. The 2026 rejections for Sign in with Apple failures look like Apple-side faults. The threads give no cause, and one says it began right after a membership renewal, so I can't tell whether an older App ID like PlantAdvocate's could be touched. An older thread shows a related mechanism: the poster says Apple hands over a person's name and email only the first time they authorize an app, and a reviewer who had used the app before hit a login failure ([Nov 2022](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/719326)). PlantAdvocate keeps the identity and the user on the server, so a repeat sign-in works, and `garden-gnome/app/routers/auth.py` treats a deleted account's leftover identity as a fresh sign-up.

On the login wall, three threads show 5.1.1 rejections of apps that required an account before anything else. One developer moved the login behind a purchase and was still flagged ([Jul 2025](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791387)). One was rejected because it required sign-up before features that are not account-based, added a guest button that left comments, profiles and favourites off, got the same rejection, and then got an Apple reply saying they believed it resolved ([Sep 2025](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/801888)). One account-dependent social app was rejected twice with the same message, and its appeal went to the App Review Board ([Jun 2026](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/835254)). None shows a final outcome in public. Two of the three say their whole value is the account, which is a weaker comparison than a plant app with a reference catalog in it. The September 2025 one is the closest match: Apple's message said non-account features sat behind a sign-up, which is the Almanac's position. Together they show reviewers do apply the rule.

**No demo account.** The notes open with a sign-in section headed as having no demo account, say there is no username, password or demo account, and tell the reviewer to tap Sign in with Apple, which makes an account with one empty indoor area called My Home. They name Google as an alternative and give the deletion path (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md`). The form guidance in the same file ticks Sign-in required and says to put `Sign in with Apple` and `none needed` in the user name and password if the form insists, and the handoff says never to give out a personal Google login. That is the right shape. Two small gaps. The notes don't say the sign-in uses the reviewer's own Apple ID, which is the fact that makes a demo account unnecessary. And Apple's field description asks for the phone number in international format with a plus sign ([reference](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/platform-version-information/)), while the file only says "with country code".

**The reviewer's path breaks.** This one is a process risk, not a design risk. As of today production's OpenAPI document lists 29 paths, among them the old `/environments/` routes and none under `/growing-areas` ([openapi.json](https://garden-gnome-api.fly.dev/openapi.json)), so step 1 of the notes would fail. The README already says to deploy first and check, and to keep the server awake. Two things are untested. The notes say the first request after idle took 6.7 seconds on 15 October (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md`), but a cold first sign-in does more: it fetches Apple's keys, creates the account, and exchanges Apple's one-time code, with 10-second timeouts on each of the two Apple calls (`garden-gnome/app/services/oauth/jwks.py`, `apple.py`) against a 20-second wait in the app (`mobile/src/auth/signIn.ts`). A typical run fits. The worst case does not, and I have not measured one. The code exchange is allowed to fail without blocking sign-in, and a retry doesn't reuse Apple's code, so the old reviewer-only failure from a server that resent it ([2020 to 2021 thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/665278)) doesn't apply. The step 5 rehearsal in the handoff uses Allison's own Apple ID, which has authorized the app before. The reviewer will be a first-time authorization, which returns the name and email and runs the code exchange for the first time. Nobody has watched that path on the production build.

**Login before the catalog.** PlantAdvocate does have significant account-based features: growing areas, plants and a care log stored on the server. So the opening sentence of 5.1.1(v) doesn't bite, and the login screen already gives its reason (`mobile/src/auth/loginCopy.ts`). The exposure is the narrower point. The Species tab and Almanac are reference content that doesn't need an account, and they sit behind it. I found no product reason for that. The comment in `garden-gnome/app/routers/species.py` says every species route requires a signed-in caller so that a new endpoint can't accidentally ship open. The notes describe the Almanac in step 6 and say nothing about why it needs sign-in. If Allison has a real reason, the notes should give it. If not, an honest sentence says the app's core is the account's own areas and plants and the Almanac is reference material inside it. I would not write that the catalog can't be shown without sign-in; it can.

**Notes and metadata.** The notes do what 2.3.1(a) asks: each feature in under a minute with its menu path, the photo limits, the hazard wording and the weather chain. They skip what Apple's help page lists for a new app: what the app is and who it's for, the business model, the devices and OS versions tested, and a regional note (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md` against the [help page](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review/)). The block is 3,987 bytes, all ASCII, against Apple's [4000-byte limit](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/platform-version-information/), so adding anything means cutting; steps 7 and 8 together take 529 bytes. The live privacy page now carries the policy effective October 16, 2026 and the live support page says there are no subscriptions ([privacy](https://plantadvocate.ai/privacy), [support](https://plantadvocate.ai/support)), so the stale pages the README recorded on 16 and 17 October no longer show on those two. I did not compare them line by line with `site/`.

**Health-adjacent claims.** Guideline 1.4.1 is written for medical apps that diagnose or treat. Hazard sentences repeat a cited source, give the source's severity for people, say to call Poison Control (US 1-800-222-1222) on a suspected poisoning, and don't triage (`garden-gnome/app/services/toxicity.py`). The listing and the review notes say they aren't medical or veterinary advice, and the website FAQ says the app is not a substitute for a vet, a doctor or poison control (`docs/app-store/listing.md`, `site/faq.html`). I found no such line on the app's own screens (`mobile/src`), so a reviewer reading 1.4.1 strictly could ask for one. The more likely friction is accuracy: the handoff records that lily of the valley is rated low by NC State and very poisonous by Missouri Botanical Garden, and left as is.

**Dormant features.** Photo check-ups and typed-symptom reading ship in the binary and appear only when the server says they're ready (`mobile/src/care/aiCapabilities.ts`). 2.5.19 names dormant features. The notes disclose both, and the privacy policy says they aren't switched on, so the features are documented, not hidden. The risk comes later: turning them on from the server after approval, without telling App Review, would be the thing the rule exists to stop.

**Account deletion.** Settings has Delete account with a confirmation, and the server asks Apple to revoke the sign-in, retrying later if Apple is slow (`mobile/src/screens/SettingsScreen.tsx`, `garden-gnome/app/routers/auth.py`). That matches [Apple's guidance](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/). I found no handler for Apple's server-to-server notifications in the backend. Apple recommends one; I found no rejection over it.

## Where the evidence is thin or conflicts

There is one self-reported approval for an Apple-only app with no credentials, and it may have been a TestFlight review. There is no 2024 to 2026 rejection I could find that rests on a missing demo account alone for such an app. Absence from a forum is weak evidence: developers post problems, not passes, and the search tool I had cannot read Reddit, where more first-hand reports probably sit. I did not confirm that reviewers sign in with their own Apple IDs from any Apple statement; it follows from how Sign in with Apple works and from two developers' accounts. The likelihoods are judgement, and the combined figure assumes independence between risks that are not independent: a broken production deploy would sink several at once. Whether a notes-only rejection can be fixed by editing the notes and resubmitting the same build, without a new binary, I did not confirm; Apple's own page says only that developers can correspond with App Review to resolve issues before [resubmitting the build](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/). I did not check the privacy label answers, the screenshots or the listing claims against 2.3, which belong to other files. Whether the 2026 Apple-side sign-in faults reach older App IDs such as PlantAdvocate's, the threads do not say.

## Fact-check, 2026-10-23

A second pass on the same day re-opened every cited source. The guidelines page, the two Apple news posts, the two App Review help pages, the App Store Connect reference, the account deletion page, the App Review distribute page, the 2025 transparency report, production's OpenAPI document and the live privacy and support pages were fetched raw and read word for word. The fourteen forum threads sit behind a browser check that refuses plain requests, so they were re-read through a summarising fetch that quoted each post. The code paths, timeouts and byte counts were checked against this branch. Most of the file held up: the 2.1(a) wording, the October 16, 2026 update date and its change list, the October 24, 2022 demo-mode sentence, the 4000-byte Notes limit and plus-sign phone format, the 50 and 90 percent review times, the over-40-percent 2.1 figure, the 9,100,620 and 2,093,244 counts, the 29 production paths with `/environments/` and no `/growing-areas`, the 3,987-byte notes block, and the dates and outcomes in the thread table.

Five things were changed. The transparency report's footnote says apps may be submitted more than once before approval, but says nothing about updates, so "and updates" came out. The help page offers a demonstration mode to apps handling sensitive information or in highly regulated industries without the word "only", so the sentence now says what it names rather than an exclusive rule; the conclusion for PlantAdvocate stands because 2.1(a) adds prior approval. The same help page does address authentication codes (put them in the Notes in advance, or expect a call, typically three to five business days), so the claim that no Apple statement covers codes was replaced with what the page says and why it doesn't fit a sign-in code from Google or Apple. The 4.8 sentence in thread 765145 is a rejection message quoted by the poster, not a reply from Apple Support, and is now described that way. Of the three 5.1.1 threads, the September 2025 one is an app with non-account features behind sign-up, not one whose whole value is the account, so that paragraph no longer says all three are; it is now named as the closest match. Two smaller wording fixes: thread 849775's proposal was an invite code that links the reviewer's own sign-in to a seeded account, and the missing Apple reply is a missing reply in the thread; thread 820537's poster cited the hidden-features rule as 2.3.1, where it lived before October 16.
