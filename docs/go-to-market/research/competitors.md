# Competitive landscape for PlantAdvocate

This file maps the iPhone apps and reference sources a PlantAdvocate user would otherwise use: houseplant and garden care apps, garden planners, plant identification and nature apps, and the pet-toxicity references people reach for. It says what each costs, how it is rated, what it claims, what its unhappy users say, and what it does about "what suits this spot", and it ends with where PlantAdvocate is different, where it is weaker, and which competitor claims it should never copy. It was checked on 2026-10-23, against the US App Store through Apple's public iTunes Search and Lookup API and the apps.apple.com pages, each product's own site, and the press and research I could open. I did not install or run any competitor, I did not cover Android, and I could not read Reddit at all.

## How far to trust the numbers

Ratings and counts are Apple's all-time US figures on 2026-10-23, and each row in the table below links to the lookup that returned it. Prices are the in-app purchase lists printed on each app's App Store page, which often show the same product name at several prices with no explanation of why, so I give the range and say which price is most common. Review themes come from the 500 most recent written reviews per app, read through Apple's public customer-reviews feed (for example [Planta, page 1](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1410126781/sortby=mostrecent/json); I read pages 1 to 10 for each app; the fact-check re-read them on the same day, and for Plant Parent and Planter the feed then returned a full 500, not the 450 first read). Written reviews skew negative compared with the star-only ratings people leave when Apple prompts them: Planta's overall rating is 4.75, while the 500 written reviews average 2.90. So the percentages below say what unhappy reviewers talk about, not how many users are unhappy.

Reddit was unavailable twice over: the search tool refused the domain and the fetch tool refused www.reddit.com, and a direct request from this machine also failed. The only Reddit-derived evidence here is one third-party aggregator that gives no date, which I flag where I use it. Press coverage of the paywall problem is thin; I found app-store review analyses, one design-complaints site and one consumer-protection submission, but no newspaper or magazine piece.

## The short version

The plant-care crowd is large, crowded and subscription-shaped. A [search for "plant care"](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=plant%20care&entity=software&country=us&limit=50) returned 46 apps, all listed Free to download, 28 of them with identifier or ID in the name, 29 filed under Education, 11 with at least 10,000 ratings and 17 with fewer than 100. In every leader I checked, a weekly, monthly or yearly subscription sits behind the free download. The leaders (PictureThis at 1.1 million ratings, PlantIn at 230,000, Plantum at 119,000, Planta at 114,000, Plant Parent at 93,000) lead with a camera and sell a subscription, and between 29% and 59% of their written 1 and 2 star reviews are about money: surprise charges, trials that billed anyway, and cancellation. None of their App Store listings names an organization or publication as the source of its care facts.

PlantAdvocate has a real gap to stand in. Nothing I read claims a per-spot verdict that says this plant does not suit this place, why, and from which published source, and nothing I read combines named sources, plain people-and-pets hazard statements where a missing record is never read as safe, no ads, no in-app purchases and no tracking. The pet-toxicity side of the market is mostly ASPCA's web list and a scatter of tiny apps, none of the dedicated ones with more than 30 ratings.

It is also very new and narrow. It has no ratings, it runs on iPhones only, photo matching exists only on phones with Apple Intelligence, its catalog is about 995 species against competitors who claim tens or hundreds of thousands of plants, it sets reminders for 129 species, it has no light meter and no frost-date planting calendar, and it asks for a sign-in before it shows anything. The claims it should never borrow are the ones the competitors lean on hardest: accuracy percentages, scan-to-know-if-it's-safe, free trials, human or AI "botanists", user counts and "no guesswork".

## The names

On 2026-10-23 no app on the US App Store is called PlantAdvocate or PlantAdvocate: Garden Care. I searched Apple's US storefront for [plantadvocate](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=plantadvocate&entity=software&country=us&limit=50), [plant advocate](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=plant%20advocate&entity=software&country=us&limit=50) and [PlantAdvocate: Garden Care](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=PlantAdvocate%3A%20Garden%20Care&entity=software&country=us&limit=50); they returned 48, 48 and 47 apps, and none has "advocate" in its name or its seller's name. A [lookup of 6792203800](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6792203800&country=us), the App Store Connect id in `mobile/eas.json`, returns nothing, as expected for an app that has never been released. A web search for the exact word PlantAdvocate found no product or company, only the general idea of plant advocacy (for example [the Plant Initiative](https://www.plantinitiative.org/learn) and [PlantAmnesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_Amnesty)), and a search for "Plant Advocate" with app or trademark terms found no product or trademark of that name; the nearest trademark filings were (not re-checked; Justia refused the fact-check's requests) [PLANT APP](https://trademarks.justia.com/984/00/plant-98400890.html), [PLANTA](https://trademarks.justia.com/974/17/planta-97417754.html) and [PLANT PARENT](https://trademarks.justia.com/973/56/plant-97356260.html).

The suffix is not unique. The search for [garden care](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=garden%20care&entity=software&country=us&limit=50) returns Planta ("Planta: Plant & Garden Care") and at least five other apps with Garden Care in the name, among them Groundskeeper: Garden Care, Plantopia: Garden Care, FloraLog: Garden Care & Water, Growli: Plant & Garden Care and YardStick: Lawn & Garden Care. The full name is still unused; what makes it distinct is the first word.

This check has limits. iTunes search lists only released apps, so it cannot see a name another developer has reserved but not shipped. A web search is not a trademark search, and I did not query the USPTO database, so this is not clearance. The finish-line handoff already names plain PlantAdvocate as the fallback if the long name is refused (`docs/2026-10-16-app-store-finish-line-handoff.md`, step 1 of the paperwork). One more overlap to know about: the app's Species Almanac shares a word with Almanac.com's [Garden Planner](https://gardenplanner.almanac.com/). I found nothing suggesting a conflict, but both sit in the same category, so it is worth a second look before the word goes into the screenshots.

## Plant-care apps

Planta is the closest in spirit and the most-rated pure care app. Its App Store subtitle is Identifier, Diagnose & Journal, and its description leads with keeping plants alive without guesswork, adds reminders that adapt to the home, local weather and plant type, a built-in light meter sold as a way to find the right spot for each plant, a symptom doctor called Dr. Planta, a plant journal, shared care and a community ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-plant-garden-care/id1410126781)). On spot-fit, that is the nearest thing in this field to PlantAdvocate's idea, but it works by measuring light with the camera and adjusting reminders, and I found no claim that it tells you a given plant does not suit a given place. Its site [names no source](https://getplanta.com/) for its advice; it says reminders use over 30 parameters plus local climate and weather data. The free tier is thin by its reviewers' account: of 236 written 1 and 2 star reviews, 126 mention money, and several say the free version gives only a watering schedule and asks for an account first. An [iMore review from 31 May 2023](https://www.imore.com/apps/planta-is-a-pricey-but-detailed-houseplant-care-iphone-app-for-indoor-gardeners) called it pricey but detailed and found setup for each plant lengthy. Sixteen of those 236 low-star reviews mention a dead or overwatered plant, and one 4-star review says the app tells the person to water and then that they are watering too much. That is a thin hint that PlantAdvocate's feel-the-soil-first check answers something real, though one review is not a trend.

PictureThis is the giant: 1.1 million ratings, and its [description](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1252497129&country=us) claims over 98% accuracy, more than 400,000 plant species, toxic plant warnings meant to keep pets, children and family safe, a light meter and round-the-clock chat with experts. Its subtitle is Plant care and identification, and its [site](https://www.picturethisai.com/) leads with identifying a plant with a snap. A 2023 [submission on Deceptive Patterns](https://deceptive.design/articles/picture-this-app-uses-deceptive-tactics-to-trick-users-into-paid-upgrades-and-makes-it-difficult-to-cancel-subscriptions), which the site says it does not verify, describes three promotion screens on opening, two of them impossible to skip, and an accidental trial that would bill automatically on day 8. [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/picturethisai.com) shows 2.0 from just 13 reviews, spanning April 2021 to September 2026, mostly about being charged after a free trial. In the 500 most recent App Store written reviews (9 July to 21 October 2026), 172 are 1 or 2 stars and 78 of those mention money. The accuracy claim is the one that outside researchers have tested; see the pet-toxicity section.

Greg is still alive and maintained (its listing updated on 2026-10-23, [with 20,698 ratings](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1512912236&country=us)). Its subtitle is Identify, Diagnose & Reminders; it calls itself a zero-guesswork care app and community, lets people scan a plant free, and then sells SUPER Greg at [$29.99 a year after a 7-day trial](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/greg-plant-identifier-care/id1512912236). Its watering advice is described as rooted in the FAO Penman-Monteith method for estimating evapotranspiration, but the only place I found that claim is a [partner article on House Plant Journal](https://www.houseplantjournal.com/?p=1835) that carries a referral link, so it is the company's own description, and I found no independent check of it. That method is a way of calculating water use, not a published source for a species' light or soil needs. Its written reviews are the worst of the big care apps after Blossom: 310 of 500 are 1 or 2 stars, 122 of those mention money, 58 mention wrong or failed identification, and a smaller but real group say their plant was not in the app.

Blossom (subtitle: Leaf & Flower Identification) is cheaper at the monthly level and dearer at the yearly one. [Mosaic's listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/blossom-plant-care-guide/id1487453649) shows yearly premium at $59.99, $69.99 or $79.99, which fits a February 2025 written review complaining that the yearly price rose from $29.99 to $79.99 (a reviewer's claim, not something I could confirm). It offers an "AI Botanist", a search that filters by light, difficulty and pet safety, and weather alerts; the listing names no sources, and the app has not been updated since 2026-03-19 per [Apple's data](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1487453649&country=us). Of 500 written reviews, 338 are 1 or 2 stars and 188 of those mention money; 129 mention cancelling, charges or refunds.

Plantum, PlantIn and Planty form the weekly-subscription cluster. [Plantum](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plantum-ai-plant-identifier/id1476047194) (subtitle: Care Guide and Identification) says it bills weekly, monthly or annually; [PlantIn](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plantin-plant-identifier-care/id1527399597) (subtitle: Disease diagnosis・advice app) lists weekly premium at $6.99 to $17.99 beside yearly $29.99 to $39.99; [Planty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plant-identifier-care-planty/id1603599822) (subtitle: Tree Identification, Ad- Free) lists weekly at $7.99 and $8.99. Plantum's low-star reviews are the most money-focused of any app I sampled (136 of 231). PlantIn is the exception to the pattern on [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/myplantin.com), which shows about 4.3 across 529 reviews (the page text and its star graphic differ slightly), with praise for support and a minority reporting unexpected charges and difficulty cancelling. Plant Parent (subtitle: Identify, Diagnose & Reminders, the same as Greg's) is the gentler Glority sibling: only 72 of its 500 most recent written reviews are 1 or 2 stars.

Vera, from Bloomscape, is a reminder-and-journal app for plants with a care library ([Bloomscape's page](https://bloomscape.com/vera/)), and the evidence on it is conflicting. That page still shows an App Store badge, but the linked [App Store page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vera-plant-care-app/id1474870193) returns a 404 and an [iTunes lookup of its id](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1474870193&country=us) returns no result, so I cannot give a price or rating and it may have left the US store. Older write-ups, which I saw only as search-result excerpts and did not re-check (for example [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/bloomscape-vera-app-plants-36824626)), disagree on whether it is free or has a three-plant free tier. An undated [Reddit-derived aggregator](https://gummysearch.com/tools/best-products/plant-app/) lists Vera at 4.0. Treat Vera as a fading competitor until someone looks on an iPhone.

"Smart Plant" is not a single app. The search for [smart plant care](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=Smart%20Plant%20care&entity=software&country=us&limit=10) returned nine apps; the only two with Smart Plant or Plant Smart in the name are UrbiGrow: Smart Plant Care with no ratings and Plant Smart with 13, and neither is established.

## Garden planners

The planners are a different customer, mostly vegetable growers, and they sell planting dates, not plant care. Seed to Spoon ([Park Seed](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seed-to-spoon-garden-planner/id1312538762)) is the best-rated planner here (4.78 from 9,290); its subtitle is Layout, Planting Calendar, and its listing promises planting dates calculated for the user's location and weather alerts for frost and heat. Planter (subtitle: Plan your vegetable garden!) is the happiest base: only 26 of its 500 most recent written reviews are 1 or 2 stars, and its [page](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planter-garden-planner/id1542642210) lists companion and combative plant information, a planting calendar, a square-foot grid and a frost-date map. GrowIt (Glority; subtitle Veggie & Fruit Planting Guide) offers a [planting calendar by location and climate](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/growit-garden-planner/id6443580320), hardiness maps and seasonal freeze warnings.

GrowVeg's Garden Planner (positioned as a tool that helps design the best layout for a vegetable garden) and Almanac.com's Garden Planner are web tools sold by subscription, [$35 a year recurring](https://www.growveg.com/subscribeinfo.aspx), and [gardenplanner.almanac.com](https://gardenplanner.almanac.com/) says it covers over 250 vegetables, herbs, fruit and flowers and adapts to the user's area using a database of over 5000 weather stations. Neither site mentions an iPhone app, and I found no GrowVeg app on the US store. VegPlotter (subtitle: Allotment & Planting Calendar) says on its [site](https://www.vegplotter.com/) that it adjusts to local climate and builds schedules from local frost data, and its own pages conflict on the free plan, which the [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vegetable-garden-farm-planner/id6695760911) caps at 20 plantings a year and the site at 40. Gardenize (subtitle: Plant identification & Garden), which [claims a 45,000-plant database](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gardenize-plant-care-gardening/id1118448120) and location-based care advice, has 272 ratings and was last updated 2025-07-17. Veggie Garden Planner's [description](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1329927332&country=us) says its sowing and harvest times are adjusted to USDA hardiness zones 7 to 8, and the app was last updated on 2024-11-09.

In the planners' written reviews, money matters less (Planter 6 of 21 low-star reviews in the first 450-review read, not re-checked against the fuller sample; Seed to Spoon 25 of 63) and lost data and crashes matter more. Two Seed to Spoon reviews, from 2022 and 2024, say an update wiped the person's garden records, and several 2020 Gardenize reviews say an update or a login lost months of entries. For a keeper of long-lived records, which is what PlantAdvocate's plants and care logs are, that is a reminder of what losing history costs.

## Identification and nature apps

Seek, iNaturalist and Pl@ntNet are free and do not sell care advice. Seek's subtitle is Identify plants & animals, and its [listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seek-by-inaturalist/id1353224144) is built around badges; iNaturalist (subtitle: Discover nature around you) [describes itself](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6475737561&country=us) as free, nonprofit and community-powered, and its newer app is rated 4.09 from 709 against 4.58 from 4,726 for Classic. PlantNet (subtitle: Plant Identification) [presents itself](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=600547573&country=us) as a citizen-science project that identifies plants living in nature. I found no source that says any of the three gives care or hazard advice, which is my inference from their listings, not a statement by them. Their complaints are about identification and bugs: 69 of 223 Seek low-star reviews are about wrong or failed identification, and at least eight PlantNet reviews from March to July 2026 report the app refusing to connect, most quoting a message that the person's IP or country has been blocked.

Apple itself is the built-in default. [Visual Look Up](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104962) identifies plants in photos on iOS 15 or later, so "what is this plant" is already answered inside Photos for most iPhone owners, with no app to install.

## Pet-toxicity references

The reference people cite is ASPCA's [list of toxic and non-toxic plants](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants), 1,024 entries on 2026-10-23. The page does not say where the information comes from or how it is reviewed; it calls the list not all-inclusive, and tells people to call a veterinarian or the ASPCA's hotline at (888) 426-4435. The [hotline page](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control) says a consultation fee may apply and mentions no app, and an [iTunes search for ASPCA](https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=ASPCA&entity=software&country=us&limit=10) returns an ASPCA insurance app and a volunteer portal but no plant-toxicity app. Pet Poison Helpline's [A to Z list](https://petpoisonhelpline.com/poisons/) holds about 328 entries across plants, medicines, foods and household items (counted from the distinct entry links on the page), lists (855) 764-7661, and states no sources either.

The apps built on top are tiny. None of the dedicated pet-toxicity apps I found has more than 30 ratings: [Toxic Plant: Pet Poison Guide](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1456505975&country=us) is 2.00 from 26 and charges for identification; [Pet Toxic](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6758074515&country=us) (5.00 from 7) says its content is written and reviewed by a licensed veterinarian using ASPCA Animal Poison Control, Pet Poison Helpline and the Merck Veterinary Manual, which makes it one of only two in this field that name sources; [Toxic Plants for Cats & Dogs](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6809958984&country=us), released 2026-09-28 with no ratings yet, says it holds 636 plants from ASPCA's published lists; and [Pet Safe Plant Identifier](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6755623945&country=us) has 5 ratings. Everyone else treats toxicity as a feature of a care app: PictureThis warns after a photo scan, [Planty lists toxicity information](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1603599822&country=us), and Blossom has a pet-safety filter.

The evidence that photo scans are a poor basis for a safety verdict is real but should be quoted carefully. A [Leeds press release of 12 April 2023](https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news-environment/news/article/5275/phone-apps-unable-to-identify-one-in-five-plants) reports six apps tested on 38 herbaceous plants native to Ireland, the best reaching 80 to 88% accuracy, and the researchers say the apps should not be trusted to identify plants with 100% accuracy, especially toxic ones. A 2022 letter in the Journal of Medical Toxicology, ["Validation of a Plant Identification Application Using Digital Images of Toxic Plants"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8938528), tested PictureThis on 369 pictures of toxic plants and found it named the correct genus in 82% and both genus and species in 64%.

## Where PlantAdvocate is genuinely different

The first difference is the spot verdict with its reason and source. A growing area lists what needs addressing among its plants, says why ("this spot gets 6+ hours of direct sun; sources record it for part shade, full shade"), and names the source, in `garden-gnome/app/services/fit.py` and as described in `docs/app-store/listing.md`. The competitors I read sell either a measurement (a light meter) or a calendar (planting dates by frost), and none claims a per-plant misfit with a cited reason.

The second is sourcing. Every care-app listing I read, and Planta's site, names no source for its care facts, and the one method named, Greg's Penman-Monteith, is a calculation. PlantAdvocate names the organization on each cited fact, labels anything borrowed from the genus, and says "not yet cited" where that is true. It also states plainly that the check schedules for 129 common plants are its own and uncited, which no competitor listing I read does about its own advice.

The third is hazard honesty. For people and pets, its hazard sentences say what the source records, give the source's severity for people only, give the US Poison Control number, and never read a missing record as safe (`garden-gnome/app/services/toxicity.py`). The dedicated pet-toxicity apps are tiny and only two name sources; PictureThis's toxicity warning is a by-product of photo identification.

The fourth is the deal. It is free with no ads, no in-app purchases and no trial, where the leaders run subscriptions and several declare tracking on their own App Store privacy labels. Of the 18 App Store pages in the table that I downloaded on 2026-10-23, the pages for [Planta](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planta-plant-garden-care/id1410126781), [PictureThis](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picturethis-plant-identifier/id1252497129), [Blossom](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/blossom-plant-care-guide/id1487453649), [Plantum](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plantum-ai-plant-identifier/id1476047194), [Plant Parent](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plant-parent-plant-care-guide/id1612792132), [PlantIn](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plantin-plant-identifier-care/id1527399597), [Planty](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/plant-identifier-care-planty/id1603599822) and [GrowIt](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/growit-garden-planner/id6443580320) carry a Data Used to Track You section, while those for Greg, Seed to Spoon, Planter, Gardenize, Veggie Garden Planner, VegPlotter, Seek, PlantNet, iNaturalist and Toxic Plant do not. PlantAdvocate's draft answers say nothing is used for tracking (`docs/app-store/app-privacy.md`), which becomes a public fact only once the label is published.

The fifth is the check-in style. PlantAdvocate asks the person to feel the soil, with Watered or Still damp both keeping the streak, where a competitor's reminder simply says water. That is a product difference that can be shown in a screenshot, though it is not proved to be better, and the demand evidence is thin: by a loose keyword match, reminder complaints are a minority theme (21 of Planta's 236 low-star reviews, 31 of Greg's 310, read from the same review feeds linked in the table above).

## Where PlantAdvocate is weaker

It is new, and a new app has no ratings. The care leaders have between 20,698 (Greg) and 1,120,755 (PictureThis) ratings, so on a results page PlantAdvocate starts with a blank where they have a 4.6 to 4.8. It has no reviews to point to, no social proof and no ranking history.

It is iPhone only. Most of the competitor listings I pulled show iPad support, and Planter lists a Mac version, while PlantAdvocate sets `ios.supportsTablet` false (`docs/app-store/listing.md`) and has no Android or web app. Photo matching exists only on iPhones with Apple Intelligence, which [Apple lists](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115), for iOS 27, as the iPhone 15 Pro and 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 models or later, iPhone Air and iPhone Duo, whereas the care apps above list minimum iOS versions between 15.0 and 18.0 (for example [Planta](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1410126781&country=us) at 18.0 and [PictureThis](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1252497129&country=us) at 15.0) and Apple's own [Visual Look Up](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104962) is built in from iOS 15. I could not find a source for what share of iPhones in use lack Apple Intelligence, so I do not give one.

Its catalog is small next to the claims around it. PlantAdvocate has 999 records in `garden-gnome/app/data/verified/`, which makes about 995 species (`docs/app-store/listing.md`); PictureThis claims over 400,000 species, Gardenize 45,000 plants, and ASPCA lists 1,024 for pets alone. A person who searches for a plant that is not among the 995 gets nothing, and the review samples show people complain about exactly that, though in only about 3% to 5% of low-star reviews. Reminders reach fewer still: only 129 of the 997 catalog rows have a check schedule (`docs/app-store/listing.md`), so a species outside those 129 gets no reminder at all.

Its pet coverage is thinner than ASPCA's list. Of the 999 verified records, 280 carry a pets-toxic verdict, 158 a pets-not-toxic verdict and 561 none (counted from `garden-gnome/app/data/verified/*.json`, field `toxic_to_pets`). For the 561, the app says there is no record, which is right, but a pet owner arriving with ASPCA's longer list in mind will often meet "no record". ASPCA is not among the authorities the catalog accepts (`garden-gnome/app/data/claims/authorities.py`), so its list is not a source here. For pets, the hazard sentence tells the person to contact a vet right away (`garden-gnome/app/services/toxicity.py`) and names no animal poison line, where ASPCA and Pet Poison Helpline run the two pet-specific lines found in the pet-toxicity section above.

It has no light meter, no frost or hardiness answer and no planting calendar. The competitors measure light with the camera; PlantAdvocate asks the person to say how many hours of direct sun a spot gets (`mobile/src/growingAreas/realEstate.ts`). The fit engine says in its header that it deliberately does not answer whether a plant survives a winter in a place (`garden-gnome/app/services/fit.py`), and a search of `mobile/src` finds no sowing calendar or bed-layout tool. The planners above sell the calendar and the frost answer and the care apps sell the light meter, so PlantAdvocate is not a garden planner and should not be marketed as one.

It has no expert chat and no community, which Planta, PictureThis and Greg each offer in some form, and photo check-ups and symptom reading are switched off in this version (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md`). It is built by one independent developer who also runs other projects, so a support promise of the kind PictureThis makes with its round-the-clock expert chat is not available.

It asks for a sign-in before it shows anything, which at least one competitor's reviewers resent, and the server sleeps when idle: a first request after idle took 6.7 seconds on 15 October (`docs/app-store/review-notes.md`), where an established app opens straight away.

## Competitor claims PlantAdvocate should never copy

Accuracy figures. PictureThis's listing says it identifies plants with over 98% accuracy, and the independent test I could open put the best of six apps at 80 to 88% on a small set of herbaceous plants. PlantAdvocate's photo path only suggests matches for the person to confirm, and it should never carry a percentage.

Scan to know whether it is safe. PictureThis says it will alert you if a plant is toxic once it has identified it from a photo, and the research above says identification is not reliable enough to bet an animal's health on. PlantAdvocate's rule that a missing record is never safe and a species is confirmed by a person is the opposite stance and the one to keep saying.

Free scans, free trials and "try it free". Greg's listing offers a free scan and then a 7-day trial; PictureThis opens with promotional screens; the money complaints above are the largest single theme in the category. PlantAdvocate's claim that it is free with no ads and no in-app purchases is its strongest, and it holds only while nothing is ever sold, so no "free trial" or "try free" wording should appear anywhere. If anything is ever charged for, the listing, the site and the Open-Meteo non-commercial terms that `docs/app-store/listing.md` flags all need revisiting first.

Human or AI experts. PictureThis, Plant Parent and PlantIn offer chat or a consultation with experts or a botanist, and Blossom an "AI Botanist". PlantAdvocate has neither, and the organizations it cites, NC State Extension, the Royal Horticultural Society, Missouri Botanical Garden and the rest, have not reviewed or endorsed it, which its description already says.

Diagnosis. Planta's Dr. Planta, PlantIn's disease-diagnosis subtitle and the Identify, Diagnose & Reminders subtitle on Greg and Plant Parent all promise a diagnosis or treatment from a photo. PlantAdvocate's photo check-ups are off, and nothing in its copy should suggest otherwise.

User counts and badges. VegPlotter's description tells readers to join over 100,000 gardeners, and Planta's description leads with an Editors' Choice line. PlantAdvocate has no users to count and no ratings, and must not invent either.

Authority by assertion. GrowIt says it was designed by plant experts and backed by years of research; Planty says it consulted expert botanists. PlantAdvocate's alternative is to name the source, so it should keep doing that and not borrow the adjectives.

No guesswork. Planta's description promises a plant kept alive without guesswork and Greg calls itself zero-guesswork. PlantAdvocate's own home page uses NO GUESSWORK in its hero strip and says "Most plant apps guess" as a section heading (`site/index.html`, lines 298 and 324). I verified only that the competitor listings name no source, not that their advice is a guess, so that heading is a comparative claim I cannot back, and the strip uses the same overpromise as two competitors. Both are worth passing to whoever writes the site copy; "sources named" and "an unknown is never a pass" are claims the product can stand behind.

## Where the evidence is thin or conflicting

I did not install or use any competitor, so every statement about what an app does is what its own listing or site says, and "none claims X" means the pages I read do not, which is weaker than saying the app cannot. Reddit was unreadable, so community sentiment rests on App Store written reviews and one undated aggregator. Prices vary: Planta's yearly premium appears at $35.99 most often but also at $47.99 and $17.99 on the same page, PictureThis was described in 2023 as $29 to $49 a year against $39.99 now, and a search summary quoting [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/greg--plant-identifier-and-care/about) (not re-checked; the site refused the fact-check's request) said Greg costs $30 a month, which conflicts with the store's $29.99 a year and which I take to be a mistake. Vera's availability conflicts between Bloomscape's page and the store. VegPlotter's free limit is 20 plantings in one place and 40 in another. The GrowVeg and Almanac planner prices are from their own subscribe pages, while a search-result excerpt of an older [Almanac support reply](https://cdn.almanac.com/comment/reply/node/123103/comment_node_blog/115641) (not re-checked) reported $29 a year, and I did not reconcile the two. The review percentages come from loose keyword matches over a sample that is neither random nor the same length of time for each app.

## Fact-check, 2026-10-23

A second pass re-opened the sources the same day and tried to refute each number, quote, rule and date. Most held. The App Store searches (48, 48 and 47 results with no "advocate" in any name or seller; 46 "plant care" results, all Free, 28 with identifier or ID in the name, 29 under Education, 11 at 10,000 ratings or more, 17 under 100), every rating and count in the apps table, the update and release dates, the minimum iOS versions, every subtitle and every in-app price except one were confirmed against the iTunes Lookup API and the apps.apple.com pages. So were the description wording quoted for PictureThis, Planta, Greg, Blossom, Pet Toxic, Planty, GrowIt, Seed to Spoon, Gardenize, Veggie Garden Planner, VegPlotter, iNaturalist, PlantNet and Toxic Plants for Cats & Dogs; the GrowVeg and Almanac prices, trial terms and the 250-plant and 5000-station claims; the Trustpilot figures for PictureThis (2.0, 13 reviews, April 2021 to September 2026) and PlantIn (4.3 in text, 4.5 on the graphic, 529 reviews); the Leeds release (12 April 2023, six apps, 38 herbaceous plants native to Ireland, best 80 to 88%, the toxic-plant warning); ASPCA's 1,024 entries, its "not meant to be all-inclusive" note, the absence of any named source and the hotline's consultation fee; Visual Look Up on iOS 15 or later; the iMore, House Plant Journal, Deceptive Patterns and Planta-site claims; VegPlotter's 20 and 40 planting limits; and the repo facts (999 verified records with 280, 158 and 561 pets verdicts, 129 of 997 rows with schedules, `supportsTablet` false, the 6.7-second wake, the hazard sentence's vet instruction, the `site/index.html` lines 298 and 324).
