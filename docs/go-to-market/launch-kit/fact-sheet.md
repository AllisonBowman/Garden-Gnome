## The fact sheet

PlantAdvocate helps you look after the places you grow, from a windowsill to a back bed. You describe each place you keep plants, and it shows what suits that spot, what doesn't, and why. What needs addressing comes with the reason and the source. Its advice is never made up on the spot.

### What it does

A growing area is a place you keep plants, such as a raised bed, a windowsill or a hanging basket, with its room, its sun, whether it is indoors or out, and what it is for. PlantAdvocate checks each plant against its spot on indoors or out, sun, soil, space and headroom, upkeep and goals. It lists what needs addressing, with the reason and the source, and puts forward good candidates: species with nothing on record against that spot and at least 1 thing confirmed for it. A blank measurement counts as unknown. Species pages show facts such as light, water, soil, temperature and size, and say plainly what a source records about harm to people or pets, with the US Poison Control number where people are at risk. A watering check asks you to feel the soil, then tap Watered or Still damp. Walk the garden turns a typed or spoken description of what's planted into plants to confirm. The Gnome, PlantAdvocate's mascot, gives care advice built by fixed rules from a species' facts and the plant's own care log.

### What it doesn't do

PlantAdvocate never calls a plant safe, and a missing record reads as unknown. Hazard notes are based on what the sources publish. They are not medical or veterinary advice. Not every fact is cited: older values are labelled "From the catalog — not yet cited", and the check schedules are PlantAdvocate's own. It doesn't diagnose a sick plant. It doesn't judge whether a plant will survive the winter where you live, and it has no hardiness-zone matching, planting dates, frost dates or bed layouts. Reminders exist only for the 129 species with a check schedule. Photo suggestions work only on iPhone models with Apple Intelligence, suggest species from the catalog for you to confirm, and have no measured accuracy.

### Sources

Researched care facts are cited to the organizations that published them, and each researched species page lists its sources, each opening the source's own page. Eight organizations are cited: NC State Extension, the Royal Horticultural Society, Missouri Botanical Garden, Clemson Cooperative Extension, UF/IFAS Extension, Penn State Extension, University of Maryland Extension and UGA Cooperative Extension. A value borrowed from a plant's genus is labelled as borrowed. Some older species data came in part from Perenual, which the app credits in Settings. Older values that no source backs yet, whether from Perenual or from PlantAdvocate's original catalog, are labelled "From the catalog — not yet cited". The Almanac's Beginner, Intermediate and Fussy grades are worked out by PlantAdvocate, not assigned by any source.

PlantAdvocate is independent. The organizations it cites have not reviewed or endorsed it.

### Privacy

PlantAdvocate has no ads, no tracking, and no analytics or crash-reporting software, and it doesn't sell data or share it with advertisers or data brokers. [Confirm against the live label:] Its App Store privacy label lists Data Linked to You as Contact Info, Identifiers, Location and User Content, and nothing under Data Used to Track You. Location (a growing area's city, region and country) and User Content are also marked Analytics for one reason only: the community census, which is off unless you turn it on and carries no name, email, notes, exact dates or coordinates. A photo used for photo suggestions is read on the phone and never uploaded. Walk the garden's dictation is handled by Apple's speech recognition, on the phone where it can and otherwise on Apple's servers, and the screen says which. PlantAdvocate never receives the audio or the transcript, only the plants you confirm. Location is optional, and weather services receive a growing area's coordinates rounded to about a kilometer. Data is stored in the United States by Fly.io. Deleting your account in Settings erases it, with its plants, care logs and growing areas, from the live database at once. Storage snapshots clear within 5 days, and a census export made before the deletion can't be called back. The policy is at https://plantadvocate.ai/privacy.

### The minimum iOS version

`mobile/app.json` sets no deployment target, so the Expo SDK 57 default applies. That default is iOS 16.4: the prebuild Podfile reads `platform :ios, podfile_properties['ios.deploymentTarget'] || '16.4'`, the generated project sets `IPHONEOS_DEPLOYMENT_TARGET = 16.4` (checked in the main checkout's prebuild of 1 August, on Expo 57.0.7), `ExpoModulesCore` declares 16.4, and the app's own `plant-id` and `plant-speech` modules pin 16.4. EAS runs the same prebuild. That is a reading of the configuration, not of a built app, because the 1.1.3 build doesn't exist yet. Once the app is live, this prints the store's own record, and its `minimumOsVersion` line should read 16.4:

```bash
curl -s "https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=6792203800&country=us" | python3 -m json.tool | grep -E '"(trackName|minimumOsVersion|price|contentAdvisoryRating|trackViewUrl)"'
```

The two requirement rows, Requires and Photo suggestions, are the only places this page names iOS. Apple's marketing guidelines say to avoid "iOS" in marketing copy, and a compatibility line is the exception the App Store page itself makes. Keep it out of everything else on the page.
