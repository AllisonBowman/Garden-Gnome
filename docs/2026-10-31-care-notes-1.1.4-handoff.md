# Care notes 1.1.4: the lengthened notes, and how to read them

This is the handoff for the branch `care-notes-1.1.4`, cut from the ship branch at `2ac7b19`. It is not merged anywhere. Allison decided on 2026-10-31 that 1.1.3 ships with the short notes and that this branch is finished alongside it, so both versions can be demoed and compared.

So far 14 of the 33 notes are lengthened and 19 are still in review. This file is rewritten each time a batch clears review, so it is always true of the commit it sits in.

## What the branch changes, and why

On 2026-10-30 the 33 curated notes that called a plant "Pet-safe", "harmless" or "safe for most pets" lost those claims. `tests/test_curated_notes.py` says why: a verdict typed into prose has no citation and cannot follow the evidence. Most of them came out of that pass at a sentence or two.

This branch writes them back up from the verified records. It changes the `care_notes` line of each lengthened species in `garden-gnome/app/data/species_catalog.json`. It adds `garden-gnome/app/data/curated_note_sources.json`, which traces every sentence to its source. It adds `garden-gnome/tests/test_curated_note_sources.py`, which pins both. Each commit carries all three together, so every commit on the branch passes its tests.

## The rule each sentence follows

Every sentence rests on one citation from that species' own record in `garden-gnome/app/data/verified/`. The trace file holds the citation's url and a verbatim part of its quote, at least 6 words long. The sentence says no more than that quote says, in PlantAdvocate's own words, and copies no more than 8 words in a row.

A note runs 3 to 6 sentences and 40 to 90 words. It says what to do: where to place the plant, how to water it as a habit and never as a number of days, and how to treat its soil, feeding, pruning and sowing where a citation covers them. It says nothing about harm in either direction, nothing about eating or medicine, and nothing about pets or children. The hazard sentence alone speaks to those. It gives no hardiness zones, no survival claims, no Almanac grades and no health claims.

## How each note was checked

Each species got two drafts, one starting from the sources and one from what a caretaker needs. A judge merged them. Three independent verifiers then read the result: one checked traceability with a script against the record, one ran the repo's three regexes and compared the note with the record's fields and schedules, and one read for voice and fit on the page. A note landed here only when all three passed it in the same round. A note that failed went to a fixer and back through all three, for up to 3 fix rounds. A note whose record could not carry 3 sentences and 40 words stayed short and was not padded.

Before a note was committed, a script checked it again against the record: the quotes, the url, the length, the barred words and the punctuation.

## The notes, side by side

These are the 14 lengthened notes, each under the short note it replaces.

**Spider Plant** (*Chlorophytum comosum*)

Short: Brown leaf tips usually mean fluoride/chloride in tap water; use filtered water.

Lengthened: Spider plant grows best in average humidity and moist soil, though it tolerates drought and dry soil. Check the soil and let it dry out briefly before you water again. When you pot or repot it, use a general-purpose potting mix.

**Calathea** (*Goeppertia orbifolia*)

Short: Fussy. Needs high humidity and distilled/filtered water; crispy edges signal low humidity or hard water.

Lengthened: You may know this plant by an older name, Calathea orbifolia. Keep the soil evenly moist, but never wet or soggy. Give it at least 60% humidity so it can thrive. Aim for a stable temperature between 65°F and 75°F. Feed it once a month during the growing season.

**Wax Plant** (*Hoya carnosa*)

Short: Slow-growing trailing vine. Blooms fragrant star-shaped clusters if given enough light and kept slightly root-bound. Never cut spent flower spurs — they rebloom.

Lengthened: Wax plant is a climber that also suits a hanging basket. Through the growing season, water moderately and consistently, letting the soil get nearly dry in between. In winter, the plant needs less water and less bright light. It loves high humidity, so try a humidifier or set the pot on a tray of wet pebbles. Use a loose, fast-draining mix rich in organic matter and a pot with good drainage. Soil kept too wet or too dry can make the leaves drop.

**Prayer Plant** (*Maranta leuconeura*)

Short: Leaves fold upward at night (nyctinasty) like praying hands — normal behaviour. Use filtered or room-temperature water; cold tap water causes leaf curl and brown spots.

Lengthened: Grow prayer plant indoors in bright light, away from strong direct sun. Too much sun will fade the colors of its leaves. Keep the soil evenly moist through the growing season, then water less so it dries out in winter. The plant needs high humidity, so keep it in a humidified room or on a tray of wet pebbles. Pot it in a soil-based potting mix. Feed it monthly in the growing season, then feed much less from autumn to late winter.

**Chinese Money Plant** (*Pilea peperomioides*)

Short: Fun to propagate — freely produces 'pups' at the base. Rotate regularly for even pancake-shaped growth. Drooping leaves = thirsty; curling = too cold.

Lengthened: Set your Chinese money plant near a north, east, or west window. In a south-facing room, set it back from the window so it gets no direct sun. Keep it out of cold drafts and always above 50°F. Water when the top few centimeters of soil start drying, checking with a finger or by lifting the pot. From April to September, you can feed every 1 to 2 months with balanced liquid fertilizer at label strength.

**Moth Orchid** (*Phalaenopsis spp.*)

Short: The easiest orchid to grow indoors. After blooms drop, cut the spike above a node to trigger a second bloom. Transparent pots help you see when roots are silver (dry) vs green (moist).

Lengthened: Moth orchids are grown in pots of a bark-based mix that drains very well. Water mature plants more often when summer is hot. Feed with a balanced fertilizer at half strength 2 times a month, or at quarter strength each week. Spring bloomers with large white flowers need 1 week of autumn nights near 55°F to start flower spikes.

**Catnip** (*Nepeta cataria*)

Short: Perennial herb. Start seeds 4-8 weeks before the final frost date. Very easy to grow indoors with full sun; pinch back regularly to prevent legginess and delay flowering.

Lengthened: This plant can turn weedy, so think about growing it in a container. It grows in full sun with at least 6 hours of direct sunlight a day. It also grows in partial shade with 2 to 6 hours of direct sun. Plant it in any soil that drains well. It copes if the soil goes dry at times.

**Bachelor's Button** (*Centaurea cyanus*)

Short: Annual (also called cornflower) with vivid blue, pink, or white blooms. Start indoors February-March. Germination: 8 days at 68-70°F; 4-6 weeks to transplant. Can also be direct sown outdoors. Excellent cut flower. Deadhead regularly to extend blooming season.

Lengthened: Bachelor's button is a cool-weather annual grown in average, well-drained soil. Plant it in beds, borders, cottage gardens, meadows, or a cutting garden. Give it full sun to light shade, since too much shade makes the stems flop. It prefers moderately moist soil but copes with little water. Avoid spots that stay wet. It tolerates poor soil and needs little or no fertilizer.

**Celosia** (*Celosia argentea*)

Short: Warm-season annual with dramatic feathery or crested flower heads. Start indoors January-April. Germination: 10 days at 70-80°F; 6 weeks to transplant. Heat-loving — do not plant out until nights stay above 60°F. The flowers hold color even when dried.

Lengthened: Grow celosia in containers and hanging baskets, on a patio, or indoors as a houseplant. Give it full sun, at least 6 hours of direct sun a day. Moist soil suits it, as long as the soil drains well. Feed it a balanced liquid fertilizer. Indoors, watch for aphids and spider mites, which you can wash off.

**Nasturtium** (*Tropaeolum majus*)

Short: Annual. Start indoors February-March. Germination: 8 days at 70-74°F; 6 weeks to transplant. Thrives in poor, dry soil — rich soil produces lush foliage and few flowers. Excellent companion plant that deters aphids.

Lengthened: Nasturtium is a warm-season annual with long stems and parasol-like leaves, grown in many kinds of gardens. Climbing types can quickly cover a fence or trellis. Choose a spot in full sun, with some afternoon shade when summers run hot. It tolerates drought. Plant it in poor, well-drained soil, since overly rich soil brings leaves at the expense of flowers. Fertilizer also adds leaves but cuts down on flowering.

**Pansy** (*Viola x wittrockiana*)

Short: Cool-season annual with familiar 'face' flowers. Start indoors November-February. Germination: 16 days at 55-65°F (one of the cooler germination temperatures); 6-10 weeks to transplant. Thrives in cool weather — declines in summer heat. Excellent for late-winter and early-spring containers.

Lengthened: Pansy is a bedding plant for cool weather. Set it where it gets full sun to part shade. Plant it in humus-rich soil that stays evenly moist yet drains well. Water it thoroughly after planting. Never let the roots sit in soggy soil.

**Petunia** (*Petunia x hybrida*)

Short: Annual with prolific, trumpet-shaped blooms. Start indoors December-March. Germination: 10 days at 70-80°F; 10 weeks to transplant. Sow seeds on the surface — do not cover (needs light to germinate). Pinch tips to encourage branching. A mainstay of summer containers.

Lengthened: Petunia is grown in beds, border fronts, patio edges, containers, hanging baskets and window boxes. It can live in heavy shade, but flowering will suffer. It grows best in well-drained, light soil that is moderately fertile. Feed it with a balanced fertilizer to support fast growth and heavy blooming.

**Annual Salvia** (*Salvia splendens*)

Short: Warm-season annual with bold spikes of scarlet (or white, purple) flowers that attract hummingbirds. Start indoors December-March. Germination: 15 days at 75-78°F; 9 weeks to transplant. Deadhead spent spikes to encourage continuous flowering.

Lengthened: Annual salvia is a bedding plant. A spot with morning sun and midafternoon shade suits it. The soil should drain very well, so use raised beds or work compost into heavy ground. Water during dry spells. Pinch back the young shoots to encourage bushier growth.

**Zinnia** (*Zinnia elegans*)

Short: Heat-loving annual that blooms prolifically. Start indoors January-April. Germination: 7 days at 68-72°F; 5-8 weeks to transplant. Excellent cut flower — the more you cut, the more it blooms. Deadhead spent flowers to extend the season. Water at the base to prevent powdery mildew.

Lengthened: Zinnia is an upright flowering annual from Mexico, in the aster family. Grow it in full sun, with at least 6 hours of direct sunlight each day. Plant it in loam or a soil rich in organic matter, with good drainage.

## How to review a note

Open `garden-gnome/app/data/curated_note_sources.json` and find the species. Each sentence sits beside its source, its url and the quote it rests on. Follow the url, find the quote and read the sentence against it.

To change a note, change it in `species_catalog.json`, in the trace file and in `PINNED` in the test, together. The test fails if any of the three drifts from the others, and it fails if a quote is not in the species' record.

## How to demo both versions

The side-by-side list above compares the two versions with nothing running, and it is the quickest way to choose.

Putting the lengthened notes on a phone takes more. The species pages sit behind sign-in, so the test console at `/ui/` cannot show them, and a development build has to sign in to an API that holds this branch. A local API needs `JWT_SECRET`, `FERNET_KEY` and the Apple keys in `garden-gnome/.env`, and this Mac has only `.env.example`. With those in place, seed and run the API from this branch's worktree, then enter its address in the Backend connection card in Settings, which development and preview builds show and App Store builds do not.

That path was not run end to end, because it needs a real Apple sign-in. The API side was tested on 2026-11-01 with throwaway secrets and a test user. A database seeded from this branch took 13 seconds, `GET /species/` refused a request with no token, and it served the lengthened notes to the signed-in user.

## How to ship it

Merge it into `care-advice-honesty` only if Allison chooses to lengthen before submitting 1.1.3. Otherwise merge it into the 1.1.4 line. A Fly deploy carries it either way, and no app build is needed, because the phone reads the notes from the API. The boot refresh rewrites the rows of a database that already exists (ADR 0010), so the first boot prints `curated text refreshed N` with N counting these notes, and phones pick up the change through the catalog generation.

The catalog version in `GET /` stays `8c520db39b21:6:1`. It hashes the verified batch files, and this branch changes none of them.

## What the branch does not do

It changes no hazard sentence, no schedule, no soil, no trait and no other row. The other curated notes keep their text. It does not touch the app, the site or the App Store listing, and it does not change a verified record.
