# Inference stops at the genus, and toxicity does not inherit at all

Filling gaps from taxonomic neighbours is how the catalog reaches useful
coverage without inventing numbers, and it works: the shade-adapted genera rule
corrected most of an impossible light distribution with no research at all. But
inference is bounded deliberately. A value may be borrowed from the species'
genus and no further — never from the family — and it is labelled
genus-inferred wherever it is shown. Toxicity is excluded entirely, in both
directions.

## Consequences

A future reader will see obvious gaps that genus data could fill and be tempted
to fill them. The toxicity exclusion is the one that matters: a genus-inferred
"non-toxic" is an invented safety verdict, which is precisely the defect we
rejected in a competitor's dataset, and inheriting it from our own code would
be no better for having been automated. Coverage on toxicity therefore grows
only by citation, and stays null otherwise — the app says it has no record,
which is true.
